1 · Permission switches now hold for Echo's own tools

Behaviour change: installs that never enabled Create and edit files or Run terminal commands lose those tools until the owner turns them on, which is what the switches always said.

Lines printed from a real Toolbox under default settings and under Offline mode. Rules for tools a turn doesn't offer (screenshots, research notebook, artifacts, cards) now leave the prompt. Owners only; guests never see the owner's settings.

A "no" stays a no

A call the user denied, or the policy blocked, isn't asked for again with the same arguments in that request.

loop

Three failures in a row

After a tool fails three times running, with any arguments, Echo is told to stop varying it and change approach.

3 · Lessons are measured against their own control

Why: a lesson used on tasks the agent passes anyway collects wins regardless. In arXiv 2610.18427 (13 Oct), self-written playbooks ranged from helpful to up to 69.6 points below no playbook, and letting the agent pick its own lessons scored 53.8 on average vs 54.1 for none. EchoSpeak's grading was already code-based; this adds the missing comparison. It is memory-based adaptation, not recursive self-improvement, and it won't show a measured benefit until the real-model ablation runs.
